# Writing rules for the report and the chat summary

The reader is a builder deciding what to do next. Write for that one person, in plain language, in their language. These rules are adapted from [Humanizer](https://github.com/blader/humanizer) (MIT), which is based on Wikipedia's guide to signs of AI writing. If the user has the humanizer skill installed, you may also run it on `report.md` in file mode when you are done.

## Always

- **No emojis**, anywhere: not in headings, labels, tables or the chat summary. Use words ("Use", "Fork", "Learn from", "Contribute", "stars", "installs").
- **No em or en dashes as connectors.** Use a period, comma, colon or parentheses. Hyphens inside words, numbers, code and URLs are fine.
- **Facts first.** Start each section with the most useful fact, not with a run-up ("Let's look at", "Here's what you need to know").
- **Every sentence adds something.** Cut sentences that only repeat or announce the previous one.
- **Numbers and names over adjectives.** "143k stars, updated last week" beats "hugely popular and actively maintained".
- **Short sentences, varied length.** Real writing mixes short and long.

## Never

- "Not X but Y" contrasts that only add weight ("It's not just a tool, it's a platform"). State the point.
- One-line closers that restate the paragraph ("That's the real win.", "Let that sink in.").
- Sayings that sound deep ("At its core", "The real question is").
- Forced lists of three when the meaning has one or two parts.
- Sales words: game-changer, seamless, powerful, robust, cutting-edge, unlock, elevate, supercharge, revolutionary, leverage.
- Bold labels on every line. Bold at most one key phrase per section.
- Hedge stacks ("could potentially", "might arguably").
- Chat leftovers in the file ("I hope this helps", "Great question", "Let me know if").
- Telling the user not to build. Describe the landscape and let them decide.

## Tone by purpose

- **launch:** neutral and direct, like a good analyst. Give the verdict and the reason in two sentences.
- **me:** practical, like a friend who knows the tools. Lead with what saves time.
- **fun:** encouraging but concrete. Point to projects worth reading and a learning path.
